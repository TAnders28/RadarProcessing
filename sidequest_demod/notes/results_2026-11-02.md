# Demod side quest — results, 2026-11-02 (ThinkCentre + N210/SBX, RX2, TX off)

The SBX gain tops out at 31.5 dB, so that's what every run used. Every capture
held its IQ in RAM only. No overflows were reported at 25 MS/s, even with the
2.5 MB socket buffer.

## 1. Wi-Fi 2.4 GHz: real frames decoded, CRC-verified

Every frame below passed its 802.11 FCS (CRC-32), so these are real decodes,
not pattern matches.

| PHY | What decoded |
|---|---|
| 802.11b CCK 11 Mbps | **AP beacons on ch 1** with SSID, DS channel 1 and a 100 TU interval. One BSS beacons under a visible SSID and a second under a hidden SSID, from the same OUI. They sit in groups about 0.45 ms apart every 102.4 ms. |
| 802.11b DSSS 1 Mbps | Probe requests from a phone/laptop, including directed probes that carry SSIDs |
| 802.11a/g OFDM 6–24 Mbps | **Beacons on ch 11** from a hidden-SSID AP (6 Mbps, DS ch 11, BI 100 TU). Also ACK, Block-Ack, RTS/CTS, and an L-LTF channel estimate (SNR 20–35 dB). |
| 802.11n HT-mixed | L-SIG only (rate/length/duration). The HT-SIG rotated-BPSK flag is detected. The HT payload isn't decoded, which accounts for most of the OFDM "FCS fail" in earlier runs. |

Channel 6 had no Wi-Fi in any run, which matches the earlier survey.

Remaining failures:

* **CCK frames, mostly ~224–248 µs, intermittently fail FCS.** This is likely
  multipath, since the receiver is a hard-decision CCK demod with no
  equalizer. A rake/DFE or a soft CCK correlator would probably fix it.
* **11n/ac payloads aren't decoded.** That needs HT-SIG, HT-LTF, 56
  subcarriers, BCC/LDPC and MIMO. Possible later, but that's a real project.
* **Long OFDM frames (>~1 ms) can lose sync.** There's no sampling-clock
  tracking, and the −46 ppm clock error below makes that worse.

## 2. LTE 700 MHz: Physical Cell IDs from PSS/SSS

Four downlink carriers were found. The PCI is 3·N_ID_1 + N_ID_2. All four are
normal CP. Each SSS result integrates 80 PSS/SSS instances (0.4 s at 6.25 MS/s).

| Carrier centre | Band (guess) | PCI | (N_ID_1, N_ID_2) | SSS score | Runner-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 731.5 MHz | B12 DL | 109 | (36, 1) | 0.50 | 0.26 |
| 739.0 MHz | B12/B17 DL | 308 | (102, 2) | 0.64 | 0.35 |
| 751.0 MHz | B13 DL | 256 | (85, 1) | 0.55 | 0.25 |
| 763.0 MHz | B14 DL (FirstNet) | 17 | (5, 2) | 0.78 | 0.22 |

The same four PCIs came back in three independent runs. Runner-ups around half
the best score are expected: different N_ID_1 values share one m-sequence half
of the SSS.

The MIB/PBCH isn't decoded, so bandwidth, SFN, operator and location are
unknown. That decode needs CRS-based channel estimates, QPSK, de-rate-matching,
a tail-biting Viterbi and CRC masks against 1/2/4 antenna ports. It's doable in
roughly 300 more lines of numpy and is the natural next step. The operator
(PLMN) isn't in the MIB anyway; it's in SIB1 on the PDSCH, which needs
PCFICH/PDCCH decoding as well. That's much heavier.

## 3. Hardware finding: the N210 reference is not locked

Using the LTE towers as a frequency standard (they hold about ±0.05 ppm), all
four cells agree that **the N210 sample/LO clock is −46 ± 1 ppm off**. The PSS
position drifted about 1 sample per 12 ms at 1.92 MS/s, and there's a
consistent +34 kHz apparent CFO at 750 MHz.

The board sensors explain it: `gps_locked: unlocked` (the GPSDO has no fix, so
check the GPS antenna) and **`ref_locked: unlocked`**. The 100 MHz VCXO isn't
disciplined to the FireFly's 10 MHz.

The Wi-Fi CFOs back this up. Every device showed +77 to +110 kHz at 2.4 GHz,
which is mostly our own +46 ppm × 2.4 GHz ≈ +111 kHz.

Why it matters for the radar side, whatever happens with this side quest:

* An absolute frequency error of about 46 ppm: 112 kHz at 2.4 GHz.
* A range/timing scale error of 46 ppm.
* Any multi-radio or GPS-timed work needs `ref_locked = True`.

Fix: get a GPS fix, or feed an external 10 MHz/PPS and select it, then
confirm `ref_locked`. After that, set `CLOCK_ERR_PPM = 0` in `sq_common.py`.

Side effect on spur masking: the 10 MHz-comb spurs come from the accurate
GPSDO oscillator, so on our axis they appear at n·10 MHz·(1+46 ppm). That's
+112 kHz at 2.43 GHz. The mask now covers both the nominal and the shifted
positions.

## Not attempted / ideas

* Bluetooth LE advertising (2402/2426/2480 MHz): GFSK at 1 Msym/s, whitening,
  CRC-24. This is light and pure-numpy friendly, and would identify the
  2.4 GHz "keyboard" blips.
* ATSC 8-VSB at ~562 MHz: pilot and segment-sync detection is easy. Full
  decoding is heavy.
* ADS-B 1090 MHz is in range, but the survey showed nothing indoors on this
  antenna.
